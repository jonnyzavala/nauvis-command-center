How to interpret this

Potential surplus: Supply exceeds recorded demand. It may still be physically routed elsewhere.

Short: Your recorded machines could consume more than your known producers could supply at full speed.

Unknown: You recorded consumption but haven't entered a matching source. The dashboard does not assume unlimited supply.

The oil figures use your refinery and cracking counts but cannot account for actual crude delivery or circuit-controlled cracking. Dedicated outputs are identified by destination, but individual input routes are not yet mapped. This means the ledger detects aggregate shortages without pretending that two disconnected belts are one connected network.

    THE THREE NUMBERS THAT MATTER

    How do I use this?

    You only need the goal. The computer should do the recipe accounting.

    30/minProduction goal

    How much finished product the factory makes each minute. For science, 30/min is a reasonable starting baseline, not an obligation.

    19Machine count

    How many machines are needed to make the item and its local ingredients. 90 red circuits/min takes 19 Assembler 2s in the self-contained block.

    900/minYellow belt capacity

    The most items one full yellow belt carries per minute, both lanes combined. Red is 1,800/min. Faster belts don't increase machine output.

    Example: 90 red circuits/min

    Start with 90 red circuits/min. The planner works backward and tells you to place:

    2 green-circuit assemblers, 5 cable assemblers and 12 red-circuit assemblers.

    19 total. Inputs: 180 iron plates, 450 copper plates and 180 plastic bars per minute. All fit individually on yellow belts.

    That doesn't mean the assemblers will always run. If storage fills or consumers stop, the machines stop. The listed count is maximum sustained capacity with adequate supplies.

    Before upgrading a belt

    Yellow900/min
    Red1,800/min
    Blue2,700/min

    If your furnaces produce only 700 plates/min, any of these belts will still carry only 700. Upgrade when the belt is saturated, not just because you've researched the next color.
